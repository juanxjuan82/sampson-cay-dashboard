# Product Requirements Document: Sampson Cay Communications Strategy Dashboard

**Status:** Draft for review  
**Product owner:** ONWRD Advisors  
**Client:** Sampson Cay Project / Yntegra Group  
**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Target release:** To be planned after PRD approval

## 1. Product summary

The Strategy Dashboard is a private communications intelligence and decision tool for the Sampson Cay Project. It combines deterministic public monitoring, uploaded Facebook and Instagram insights, and the current PR activity tracker. It gives the client a short, evidence-led view of what changed, what deserves attention, and which communications action should happen next.

The primary audience to influence is Exuma and the broader Bahamian public. International pickup is a secondary objective because international coverage and high-reach voices can amplify either side of the public narrative.

The dashboard supports three communications pillars:

1. Economy
2. Environment
3. Community

Its primary outcomes are increased public trust, correction of material misinformation, and clearer public understanding of the project's plans and benefits.

The product must never present criticism as false merely because it conflicts with the project's position. It must attribute claims, distinguish fact from opinion or prediction, and ground any proposed counterpoint in an approved source.

## 2. Problem

The current dashboard exposes a large amount of data without consistently converting it into a useful decision. Social results, PR activity and public criticism live in separate sources. Repeated or syndicated opposition material can appear larger than it is. Raw engagement figures can be mistaken for public trust. Narrative summaries can become long enough to be skipped.

The client needs to know:

- What is new since the last visit?
- Is it a genuinely new issue, a repeated claim, or wider pickup of an existing issue?
- Which of the three project pillars is affected?
- Is a response warranted?
- What evidence supports a response?
- What content should be produced next?
- Did previously approved content perform or gain pickup?

The agency needs an admin workflow that preserves deterministic monitoring while using AI only for private analysis and suggestion drafting. Nothing generated by AI reaches the client until an administrator edits and publishes it.

## 3. Goals and success criteria

### 3.1 Product goals

- Make the dashboard useful in under two minutes per visit.
- Turn monitoring into one to three clear decisions rather than a news feed.
- Give every recommendation a traceable source and approved evidence.
- Present Facebook, Instagram and PR performance using the AMEC measurement structure.
- Create a repeat-use loop based on meaningful change, action and measured follow-through.
- Detect and alert on new or materially changed content every six hours.
- Support Bahamian-first communications while identifying credible opportunities for international pickup.
- Preserve human editorial and legal judgment.

### 3.2 Initial product success measures

The first 60 days after launch should track:

| Measure | Initial target |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly active client users | At least one client stakeholder in 75% of weeks |
| Briefing usefulness | At least 60% of weekly visits lead to a link view, filter action or recorded decision |
| Time to first useful finding | Under 30 seconds from Overview load |
| Recommendation action rate | At least 40% of published recommendations marked approved, planned or declined |
| Evidence completeness | 100% of published recommendations have at least one approved evidence source |
| Monitoring freshness | 95% of scheduled runs complete within one hour of schedule |
| Monitoring transparency | 100% of source failures shown with last successful check |
| Editorial control | Zero AI drafts visible to clients before explicit publication |
| Alert duplication | Zero repeat emails for an unchanged item and unchanged status |
| Measurement honesty | Zero engagement metrics described as trust, belief or unique total audience |

These are product and operational measures. Public trust requires separate outcome research; it cannot be inferred from clicks, reach or sentiment classification.

## 4. Users and permissions

### 4.1 Client user

Can:

- View the Overview, Facebook and Instagram tabs.
- Change the reporting period and available chart filters.
- Open source and post links.
- See published recommendations and their supporting evidence.
- Mark a published recommendation as approved, planned, completed or declined, with an optional note.
- Request a monitoring refresh, subject to cooldown.
- Suggest a source for administrator review.
- See monitoring freshness and disclosed coverage gaps.

Cannot:

- Change crawler rules or activate sources.
- See private AI output, private context, drafts, tokens, raw source captures or internal notes.
- Publish or edit agency recommendations.
- Change email recipients.

### 4.2 Administrator

Can:

- Perform all client actions.
- Upload Facebook CSV, Instagram CSV and the PR activity workbook.
- Add, edit, pause and test monitoring sources.
- Configure recipient emails and alert rules.
- Upload a context document or paste context for an AI analysis run.
- Manage the approved project evidence bank.
- Run AI analysis on a defined evidence set.
- Edit, save, publish, replace or withdraw a recommendation.
- Resolve duplicates and link related monitoring items into a claim cluster.
- Classify items and recommendations by pillar, audience and response type.
- Review source versions, collection failures and audit history.

### 4.3 System process

The scheduled collector can fetch public sources, calculate deterministic differences, store versions, classify by explicit rules, enqueue video transcription, and send rule-based alerts. It cannot generate or publish narrative recommendations automatically.

## 5. Information architecture

The client experience contains exactly three primary tabs.

### 5.1 Overview

The Overview is the default page and must answer the most important questions before showing detailed charts.

#### A. Your briefing

Show no more than three priority cards. Each card contains:

- Plain-language headline
- New, changed or continuing badge
- Pillar: Economy, Environment or Community
- Why it matters in one sentence
- Evidence count and source type
- Recommended action: Watch, Clarify, Show evidence, Address concern, Seek review
- Link to the issue detail
- Published agency recommendation, when available
- Client decision control

Priority is deterministic using configurable inputs such as recency, number of distinct credible publishers, material text change, named project entities, active legal/safety terms and administrator escalation. AI never sets the priority score.

If there are no priority developments, show a useful quiet-state message, the last successful monitoring time and the most recent progress item.

#### B. Narrative map

Show current issue clusters rather than a flat headline stream. Initial clusters include:

- Environmental and marine impact
- Dredging, seawall and dock
- Scale and density
- Safety and navigation
- Government approvals and legal proceedings
- Community consultation and local voice
- Jobs, training and business opportunity
- Transparency and project facts
- International campaign activity

For each cluster show:

- New items
- Distinct publishers
- First seen and latest change
- Claim status distribution
- Pillar
- Response status
- Trend over the selected period
- Direct links to all evidence

Counts must distinguish original publication, syndicated copy, social repost and independent reporting.

#### C. Three-pillar progress

A compact Economy / Environment / Community view connects communications activity to the project goal:

- What the project communicated
- How audiences responded
- Evidence of understanding or change, if separately measured
- The next evidence gap or action

#### D. PR performance

Use concise AMEC-guided sections:

- **What we put out:** releases, statements, interviews, editorials, notices and other activity.
- **What was published:** earned coverage by publication and type, message inclusion, source mix and potential exposure where valid.
- **How audiences responded:** available responses such as shares, clicks, inquiries or petition/activity indicators, each clearly sourced.
- **What changed:** survey or stakeholder evidence of awareness, understanding, trust, intent or behaviour. Display “Not measured yet” when unavailable.
- **What to do next:** one or two evidence-led recommendations.

The PR tracker is the activity and coverage source. Its categories must remain distinct. The product must not add releases, advertising, public notices and earned editorial coverage into one misleading “media coverage” total.

#### E. Decisions and follow-through

Show outstanding client decisions and the results of completed ones. Each item records owner, state, decision date, expected publication, published content link and later performance evidence.

### 5.2 Facebook

The Facebook tab displays only Facebook data and preserves Meta export definitions.

Required modules:

- Reporting period and data freshness
- Posts published
- Total post reach, labelled as the sum of post-level reach and not unique people
- Views
- Reactions, comments and shares
- Link clicks when the export actually provides them
- Total clicks kept separate from link clicks
- Median performance and distribution, with outliers identified
- Trend by publish date
- Performance by Photos, Reels and Links
- Pillar performance
- Strongest repeatable content
- Content needing adjustment
- Sortable post table with direct links

Comments are not a success target when comments are intentionally disabled. The interface must never claim Stories were published unless they exist in the uploaded data.

### 5.3 Instagram

The Instagram tab displays only Instagram data.

Required modules:

- Reporting period and data freshness
- Posts published
- Total post reach, labelled as non-unique across posts
- Views
- Likes, shares, comments, follows and saves
- Median performance and distribution
- Trend by publish date
- Performance by Reel, Image and Carousel
- Pillar performance
- Strongest repeatable content
- Content needing adjustment
- Sortable post table with direct links

Shares and saves should be prominent because they indicate active response, while remaining AMEC outtake measures rather than proof of trust.

## 6. AMEC measurement model

The dashboard uses AMEC's stages internally and accessible labels externally.

| AMEC stage | Dashboard label | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | What we are trying to achieve | Public trust, misinformation correction, three-pillar education |
| Activities | What we did | Research, content production, media relations |
| Outputs | What we put out / what was published | Posts, releases, placements, reach, message inclusion |
| Outtakes | How people responded | Views, saves, shares, clicks, inquiries, recall |
| Outcomes | What changed | Understanding, trust, preference, intent, advocacy |
| Impact | Progress toward the public objective | Sustained trust, participation, registrations or other agreed goals |

Rules:

- Every displayed metric has a stage, source, time basis and plain-language definition.
- Output and outtake metrics cannot be relabelled as outcomes.
- Sentiment or tone is a content classification, not proof of audience opinion.
- Social metrics remain platform-specific.
- Lifetime post metrics are labelled as lifetime snapshots.
- A period filter selects posts by publication date; it does not imply that all engagement occurred in that period.
- Outcome cards show “Not measured yet” unless supported by a survey, structured stakeholder research or defined first-party outcome measure.
- The dashboard should recommend a lightweight recurring trust/understanding pulse survey as a future data source.

## 7. Monitoring scope

### 7.1 Named actor and core sources

The primary configured opposition actor is Save Exuma Alliance (SEA). The source record may note the administrator-provided relationship to Turtlegrass Resort and Bob Coughlin for internal context. Client-facing content should use sourced, attributable descriptions.

Initial direct-source monitoring:

- saveexumaalliance.org and its relevant pages
- SEA Facebook
- SEA Instagram
- SEA YouTube
- SEA TikTok
- Turtlegrass Resort public site and identified public channels
- Change.org petition and updates associated with the campaign
- Small Islands Coalition / linked campaign sites when they publish Sampson Cay material
- sampsoncayproject.com as the official project fact source and change trigger

Initial earned and news-source watchlist, based on the supplied tracker:

- The Tribune
- The Nassau Guardian
- Eyewitness News
- Our News
- Bahamas Local
- ZNS Bahamas
- Bahamas Press
- Additional radio, podcast and video pages already recorded in the tracker

Search discovery should also find new public pages that mention configured entities and terms. Newly discovered domains stay pending until an administrator approves them as recurring sources.

### 7.2 Collection schedule

- Scheduled run every six hours.
- Manual refresh from admin.
- Client refresh request subject to cooldown and collected by the same deterministic pipeline.
- Overlap lock prevents concurrent collection.
- Each source shows scheduled time, last attempt, last success and current health.
- A failed source can never be summarized as “nothing new.”

### 7.3 Deterministic collection

The collector must:

1. Fetch the configured public URL through an approved method.
2. Record response status, redirect chain, collection time and content type.
3. Extract primary article/post content using source-specific selectors or a documented generic fallback.
4. Remove scripts, navigation, cookie notices, forms and standard footer text.
5. Normalize whitespace and known non-content fields.
6. Calculate a content hash.
7. Store a bounded capture and normalized text.
8. Compare with the last successful version.
9. Create an event only for a new item or material normalized-content change.
10. Run explicit entity, phrase, pillar and content-type rules.
11. Apply canonical URL and content-similarity deduplication.
12. Link likely syndications while preserving every source URL.
13. Enqueue transcription or OCR where supported.
14. Evaluate deterministic alert rules.
15. Record the complete run and item-level failures.

No language model participates in discovery, fetching, change detection, deduplication, priority calculation or alert sending.

### 7.4 Video and image processing

Version one includes video.

- Capture title, description, publish date, channel, URL and available captions.
- Prefer publisher-provided captions.
- If captions are unavailable and the file is legally and technically accessible, produce a machine transcript.
- Label transcript origin and confidence.
- Preserve timestamps for quoted or reviewed sections.
- Do not treat a low-confidence transcript as verified wording.
- Extract visible text from images or video frames only when OCR is configured; label it as machine-extracted.
- A media item's transcript update is a source change and requires editorial review.
- Client-facing recommendations may quote only text that an administrator has checked against the source.

### 7.5 Claim records

Each monitored item can contain one or more claims. A claim record includes:

- Exact excerpt or timestamped transcript segment
- Speaker or publisher
- Source link
- Publication time if available
- First collected and last changed times
- Type: factual assertion, opinion, prediction, question or call to action
- Pillar and issue cluster
- Status: unreviewed, supported, contradicted by approved evidence, disputed, needs verification or opinion
- Evidence links
- Editorial notes
- Related/syndicated claim cluster
- Response state

The system must not automatically label a claim “misinformation.” That status requires administrator review and supporting evidence.

## 8. Project evidence bank

Sampsoncayproject.com is the initial source of approved project positions. Its content should be imported as source records rather than treated as universal proof.

Each evidence record includes:

- Title and concise fact statement
- Exact source excerpt
- Source URL and document name
- Page or section
- Source owner
- Publication/effective date
- Last checked date
- Pillar
- Topic tags
- Evidence type: official project statement, study, government record, approval, plan, expert report, independent data or other
- Status: draft, approved for public use, superseded or needs verification
- Qualification: completed result, current condition, commitment, projection or proposal
- Approved wording and prohibited overstatement
- Reviewer and review date

Requirements:

- Recommendations can use only evidence approved for public use.
- Project claims with projected figures must retain the projection language.
- Changes to an evidence source flag dependent private and published recommendations as needing review.
- Administrators can upload supporting documents and add manual citations.
- The system stores a versioned audit trail.
- Evidence records link Economy, Environment and Community material into the three-pillar view.

## 9. AI analysis and suggestion workflow

AI is available only in admin and only on explicit request.

### 9.1 Inputs to an analysis run

The administrator selects:

- One or more monitored claims or issue clusters
- A defined time range
- Approved evidence records
- Optional uploaded context document
- Optional pasted context
- Desired audience: Exuma, broader Bahamas or international
- Desired format: social post, video, FAQ, web update, media pitch, statement, op-ed, briefing or watch recommendation

Uploaded or pasted context remains private and is excluded from client exports.

### 9.2 Required AI output

The AI draft must return structured fields:

- Issue summary
- What is known
- What remains uncertain
- Recommended response mode: Watch, Clarify, Show evidence, Address concern or Seek review
- Reason for responding now or waiting
- Primary audience
- Pillar
- Content concept
- Suggested headline/hook
- Key points
- Recommended format and channel
- Supporting evidence citations
- Claims needing verification
- Tone check
- Risk or counsel-review flag
- Optional draft copy

The instruction set requires calm, specific, factual language. It must avoid personal attacks, speculation about motive, repetition of inflammatory language and unsupported counterclaims. It should address the strongest reasonable form of a concern.

### 9.3 Human control

- AI output is always created as a private draft.
- The administrator can edit every field.
- Save and Publish are distinct actions.
- Publishing requires at least one approved evidence citation and a completed verification check.
- A source or evidence change makes the recommendation stale.
- A stale recommendation remains visible with a warning until replaced or withdrawn, according to configured policy.
- Every generate, edit, publish, replace and withdraw action is audited.
- AI drafts and private context never appear in the client API or export.

## 10. Recommendation design

A client-facing recommendation uses a concise, consistent card:

- **What changed**
- **Why it matters**
- **What we recommend**
- **Content to create**
- **Evidence to use**
- **Audience and channel**
- **Decision needed**
- **Source links**

A card should fit without scrolling on a typical desktop where possible. Detail and evidence open progressively.

Example:

**Concern:** Local economic benefit is unclear.

**Recommended content:** Publish a 60-second profile of an Exuma supplier showing the contracted work, the people involved and how other local businesses can register interest.

**Evidence:** Approved supplier information, opportunity registration link and consent from the featured business.

**Why now:** The concern appeared in two distinct publications this week and the project has current first-party evidence that can answer it directly.

## 11. Engagement and repeat-use loop

The product should create anticipation through useful change and closure:

1. The six-hour monitor detects a meaningful change.
2. The user receives one concise alert or sees “New since your last visit.”
3. The Overview reveals up to three prioritized developments.
4. The client reviews evidence and records a decision.
5. The completed action links to published content.
6. Later social or PR data shows the result.
7. The dashboard records the issue history and next step.

Features:

- New-since-last-visit count
- Weekly win backed by evidence
- Visible completion progress for open decisions
- Followed issue clusters
- “You approved this; here is what happened” follow-through
- Saved filters
- Calm quiet states

Avoid streaks, artificial urgency, random rewards and unsupported “narrative won” scores. The utility should feel current and consequential without manipulating the user.

## 12. Alerts and email

### 12.1 Recipient administration

Administrators can add, verify, pause and remove email recipients. Each recipient can receive:

- Scheduled digest
- Immediate high-priority alerts
- Source-failure alerts
- Weekly strategy summary

Recipient changes are audited. Client users cannot change recipients.

### 12.2 Alert rules

Alert generation is deterministic. Example triggers:

- New item from a core SEA or Turtlegrass source
- Material change to an existing item
- New legal, approval, safety or work-stoppage term
- First appearance of a configured claim
- Pickup by a new distinct publisher
- International publication or configured high-reach account
- Video transcript containing a configured term
- Official project fact-page change
- Repeated collection failure

Each email includes:

- What changed
- Source and direct link
- Publication/collection time
- Detected pillar and terms
- Whether it is new, changed, syndicated or unresolved
- Link to the dashboard
- Coverage caveat where needed

Emails do not contain AI analysis or unpublished suggestions. Unchanged items do not generate repeat alerts. The default is one daily digest plus immediate alerts for high-priority rules.

## 13. Data imports

### 13.1 Facebook and Instagram

- Accept one or more Meta CSV exports per platform.
- Detect platform from schema, not filename alone.
- Validate headers, dates and numeric fields.
- Preserve Post ID and permalink as identity.
- Deduplicate overlapping exports using platform and Post ID.
- Retain the latest lifetime snapshot for a post while preserving import history.
- Display declared export coverage separately from post publish dates.
- Report rejected rows and missing columns.
- Keep Facebook and Instagram metrics separate.
- An upload in admin updates the relevant client tab after successful validation.

### 13.2 PR tracker

- Accept the current XLSX workbook or a Media Mentions CSV export.
- Use Media Mentions as the coverage dataset.
- Use Press Releases and the editorial/letters/print sheet as activity datasets.
- Preserve worksheet meaning; do not collapse all rows into earned coverage.
- Retain HTTP(S) hyperlinks from Excel cells.
- Validate required fields and report incomplete records.
- Deduplicate by normalized outlet, date, headline and canonical URL.
- Support the tracker’s current common sources as seed candidates for monitoring.
- An upload replaces the active tracker snapshot only after validation succeeds.

## 16. Technical architecture

Target components:

- Static or application client for the three dashboard tabs
- Authenticated admin application
- Scheduled Worker or equivalent collector
- Durable database for sources, runs, items, versions, claims, evidence, recommendations, recipients and audit events
- Object storage where bounded media or uploaded context retention is required
- Email provider for transactional alerts
- Approved AI endpoint for private analysis
- Transcript/OCR service with provenance and confidence metadata

Security requirements:

- Separate admin and client roles
- Server-side authorization on every private endpoint
- Secrets stored outside source control

- Retention controls for uploaded context and raw captures
- Audit log for editorial and recipient actions

## 18. Acceptance criteria

### Monitoring

- A scheduled collection begins every six hours.
- New and materially changed content produce distinct versioned events.
- An unchanged page produces no duplicate event or email.
- A failed fetch shows the failure and last success.
- Syndicated copies are grouped without deleting source links.
- Source changes are reproducible from stored normalized text and explicit rules.
- SEA website, configured SEA social accounts, Turtlegrass sources, the core media watchlist and sampsoncayproject.com are represented in source health.
- Video records contain captions or labelled transcripts where available.

### AI and editorial control

- No AI call occurs during scheduled collection.
- An admin can upload a document or paste context into an explicit analysis request.
- The request sends only the selected source evidence and context.
- Generated content is private by default.
- A client account cannot retrieve a draft through the UI, API or export.
- Publication fails without approved evidence.
- Changed source/evidence flags dependent recommendations.
- Published copy preserves the administrator’s edits exactly.

### Dashboard

- Client navigation contains Overview, Facebook and Instagram.
- Overview opens with at most three actionable priority cards.
- Facebook and Instagram each show their own filters, charts and post tables.
- PR activity and public narrative remain on Overview.
- Every number exposes definition, source and date basis.
- Trust is shown as unmeasured unless outcome evidence exists.
- The supplied sample CSVs and XLSX import without invented Stories or merged platform totals.
- Links open the original monitored item or social post.
- Mobile and keyboard flows cover every primary task.

### Alerts

- Admin can add and verify recipients.
- Daily digest and immediate-rule preferences work independently.
- Each alert states why it fired.
- No unchanged item sends a repeat alert.
- AI text is absent from automated emails.
- Failed deliveries are visible and retry safely.

## 20. Out of scope for the first release

- Automatic posting to social media
- Automatic rebuttals or replies
- AI-controlled monitoring, prioritization or email alerts

## 21. Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Platform access becomes unavailable | Source health, approved APIs/providers, manual link intake and explicit coverage gaps |
| Repeated content inflates importance | Canonicalization, similarity grouping and distinct-publisher counts |
| AI invents a fact | Approved-evidence retrieval, required citations, verification checklist and human publication |
| Official project statement is disputed | Label source type and status; attach independent or regulatory evidence where available |
| Projection is presented as achieved | Evidence qualification carried into every draft and published card |
| Legal matter is mishandled | Seek-review response mode and configurable legal-term gate |
| Alert fatigue | Daily digest default, immediate rules limited to material triggers and deduplication |
| Client mistakes engagement for trust | AMEC labels and separate outcome measurement |
| Video transcript is wrong | Confidence/provenance labels and administrator verification before quotation |
| Quiet crawler failure looks reassuring | Last attempt, last success and failure state always visible |
| International amplification overwhelms local goals | Bahamian-first audience tag and separate international pickup objective |
